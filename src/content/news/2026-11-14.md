America's 250th birthday!  One way to celebrate patriotism this year is to discover and honor an ancestor who served during the American Revolutionary War (1775 - 1783).

Who was a Revolutionary War patriot?  Patriots were soldiers, sailors, marines, militiamen and women. They also served in noncombat roles.  Patriots were signers of foundational documents, public office holders, participants in the Boston Tea Party, civil servants, camp followers, spies, couriers, cooks, nurses, launderers, enslaved individuals, suppliers of clothing and equipment, and they managed farms and businesses in a patriot's absence.   No act of patriotism was too small.  Handing a cup of water or a blanket to a passing soldier were patriotic acts that advanced the cause of the Revolutionary War.

Where can you locate a Revolutionary War patriot's service record?  The DAR Genealogical Research System is a wealth of information that lists patriots and direct lineal descendants.  Genealogy databases, the Vining Family Association Online Genealogy, muster rolls, service/pension records, diaries and letters, family bibles and town vital records, land deeds, probate wills, town hall meeting minutes/records, and cemetery records, provide additional avenues for research.

The following not for profit societies list patriots, their service and descendants:

National Society of the Daughters of the American Revolution (NSDAR)<br>
National Society of the Sons of the American Revolution (NSSAR)<br>
National Society of Children of the American Revolution (NSCAR)

The societies are dedicated to honoring and promoting patriotic service.  Members preserve and research history, volunteer in veterans hospitals, serve meals to veterans, place flags and markers on patriot graves, send holiday greetings and care packages to veterans stationed overseas.  These are some of the many ways veterans are remembered and honored.

Do you know of a deceased patriot with an unmarked military grave?  Or a veteran whose service was overlooked and did not receive service medals or a pension?  The NSDAR and NSSAR have been instrumental in petitioning the government to recognize patriots by placing markers on their graves, correcting service records and awarding medals.   How exciting would it be to add an ancestor's name to a database!

In the 250th year of America's birth, and especially this November, let us discover and honor our patriots.
